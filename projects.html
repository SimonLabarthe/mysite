myRpackages

FORTHCOMING ABSTRACT

This article introduces myRpackages, a curated collection of R functions designed to enhance efficiency in data analysis, automated reporting, and AI-supported text classification. Researchers frequently face repetitive tasks in data preparation and output generation. The package addresses this by offering straightforward functions for creating publication-ready LaTeX/PDF tables and integrating with AI APIs for content coding. The core functions, create_descriptive_table(), create_frequency_table_2vars(), create_frequency_table_3vars(), and categorise_api(), are presented with practical examples obviously coming from starwars. By automating these common analytical workflows, myRpackages allows researchers to focus on substantive interpretation rather than technical implementation. The package is open-source and available on GitHub.

Predicting FIFA26 results

FORTHECOMING ABSTRACT

This project develops and evaluates statistical learning models to predict home team goal counts in international football matches, with specific application to the 2026 FIFA World Cup. Using a comprehensive dataset of over 50,000 international matches from 1990 to 2026, I integrate historical performance metrics, Elo ratings, geographical features, and country-level socioeconomic indicators.

Do left-wing or female representatives reduce violence against women? RDD evidence from Brazil

2026.06.15 ABSTRACT

A growing set of literature explores the effect of the election of female can didates on social outcomes without taking ideology into account. This essay aims to fulfill this gap by examining whether political affiliation contribute—alongside, and cumulatively—to the reduction of Interpersonal Violence against Women(IPVAW). Combining data from the electionsBR package and DATA SUS/SINAN on brazilian municipalities between 2016 and 2025, the article follow a sharp Regression Discontinuity Design (RDD) to explore whether barely winning elections have an impact on the IPVAW rate. The results reinforce Bochenkova et al. (2023) previous findings : gender matters more, while political orientation has limited effects.

Militarised Interstate Dispute Predicted Probabilities Dataset

2026.05.15 ABSTRACT

This paper introduces a novel public dataset providing ex ante predicted probabilities of Militarized Interstate Dispute (MID) onset at the dyadic and monadic levels for the period 1992–2012. While existing quantitative research on political violence typically relies on realized conflict outcomes as explanatory variables, theoretical frameworks emphasize the importance of anticipated conflict risk in shaping state behavior regarding arming, alliance formation, and concessions. To address this gap, I estimate four statistical learning classifiers (logistic regression, elastic net, random forest, and gradient boosting) using 91 predictors. The random forest model achieves the strongest discriminative performance on a held-out test set (AUC = 0.994, precision = 0.889), substantially outperforming parametric alternatives. The resulting dataset offers researchers a theoretically grounded measure of latent conflict propensity that can be incorporated into models of militarization, preventive war, nationalist sentiment, and related phenomena.

Economic Inequality on Democratic Backsliding: a Scoping Review

2026.02.01 ABSTRACT

Background: Rising economic inequality and democratic backsliding are two defining trends of the 21st century. While a growing body of research examines their relationship, it remains unclear how economic inequality is conceptualized, measured, and empirically linked to democratic backsliding across the literature.

Objectives: This study maps the existing empirical research on the relationship between economic inequality and democratic backsliding published before 2026. 3 research questions are asked : (1) what patterns exist between the publications related to economic inequalities and democratic backsliding; (2) how does the empirical literature conceptualize, measure, and analyze this relationship.

Using a scoping review methodology, the paper identifies dominant theoretical frameworks, data sources, methodological approaches, and reported empirical patterns. Rather than adjudicating causal claims, the review clarifies where scholarly consensus exists, where findings diverge, and where significant research gaps remain.

Methods: The Eligibility Criteria compelled empirical examination of economic, income, or wealth inequality as an explanatory variable of democratic backsliding, using quantitative, qualitative, or mixed-methods approaches. Additionally, the paper needs to be published in English in peer-reviewed journals between 2000 and 2025. The systematic Web of Science and Scopus research produced 552 articles among which I retained 24 journal articles for manual coding. I then used mixed-methods to detect patterns in literature through quantitative methods, before conducting an in-depth analysis.

Results: The 24 selected articles were published between 2012 and 2025 with 50% published after 2022. Most of the articles were conducted in North America (37%), Asia (29%) and Europe (29%). A huge majority was based on an economics (42%) or political sciences (33%) point of view, favourising statistical approaches (92%). Whereas the operationalisation of economic inequalities and democratic backsliding was diverse and acknowledge a causal positive relation between the two, studies often adopted an occidental point of view potentially misleading.
